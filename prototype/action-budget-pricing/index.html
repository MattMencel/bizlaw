Pricing the Action Budget

A throwaway model of one BizLaw Simulation, run thousands of times, to find prices where a Team that works the case can plausibly reach Par and a Team that hoards and dumps plausibly cannot. Nothing here is the game. It is a spreadsheet with a story.

The question this answers — #277. Every number in the economy is open: how big the Action Budget is, what each Action costs and how long it takes to land, how far a Client can be moved, and where the Reaction Bands sit along that movement. The map said twice that this needs a playable thing rather than a conversation. So: set the prices on the left, press Run the sweep, and read whether the prices separate good play from bad.
  • All money is in bound-units. A Client's bound — the total distance their reservation point can travel in one Simulation — is 1.00. Every settlement number and every shift is a fraction of it. That is the Case format's own spine, so the sweep is scale-free: it never has to guess dollars.
  • Collaboration is not modelled. It is a per-student dimension and this model has no students in it. Rubric totals below are out of 80, not 100.
  • Settlement quality ramps from an authored, immobile floor — Par minus a quarter of the bound — to full 40 at Par and beyond. Deliberately not the live reservation point: that number is moved by the opposing Team's Exhibits, so ramping from it would set one Side's grading scale by how hard the other Side worked. The spread is a knob; widening it hands a Team that never worked the case most of the forty points for turning up.
  • A Side does not know its own Client's reservation point. Added for #280. #277's model handed every archetype the true number every Day, which is exactly the number a Consult is charged for — so no Reaction Band threshold could ever move a result. A Side now carries a belief: it counts the blows it can see land (#270 makes service visible and its direction legible) but cannot see their size, so its picture drifts, and a Consult is what corrects it.
  • Arbitration is final-offer. Also #280. The award is whichever of the two last committed Offers sits closer to the merits, never a split between them. Splitting the difference pays extremity, and once the reservation point is private that makes refusing to negotiate the dominant strategy.
  • The exchange half is an absolute number of points, not a fraction of the Budget. Two points buys one Offer and one Exhibit riding it, and that ceiling is the entire brake on the dump. Held as a fraction it silently couples to the Budget — a fifth of ten is safe, a fifth of nine is 1.8 and the game collapses.

The sweep

Each archetype below plays a full Simulation against a Team that works the case, over many seeded runs. What matters is not any single number but the gap: the case-worker should land near Par and the hoarder should not.

Sensitivity: one price at a time

Sweeps a single price across a range and reports the separation at each value — the fastest way to see which numbers the design is actually sensitive to and which are free.

Watch one Simulation
