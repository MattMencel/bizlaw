# Copy against contract law: is what the game says legally right?

Resolves [#387](https://github.com/MattMencel/bizlaw/issues/387), part of the map [#384](https://github.com/MattMencel/bizlaw/issues/384). Checked against `origin/main` at `99fd88c`. The string list comes from the copy inventory ([#385](https://github.com/MattMencel/bizlaw/issues/385), branch `research/copy-inventory`, taken at `0c814ef`). No copy source changed between those two commits.

## Scope and method

**The question.** Does the fiction and interface copy state US contract and settlement law correctly, at the level of a college business law course? This covers the terms of art the game uses, and the refusal sentences that state a rule. Voice is out of scope; that is [#386](https://github.com/MattMencel/bizlaw/issues/386).

**What I read.** The inventory, then the strings themselves: `config/locales/reads.en.yml`, the Svelte literals under `app/frontend`, `db/cases/reference.yml` and `lib/demo/seed.rb`. `CONTEXT.md`, ADRs 0005 and 0007, `app/services/offers/accept.rb` and `app/reads/terms_board.rb` show what the mechanics actually do. I used them to separate an intended divergence from an accidental one.

**Sources.** I used primary sources only: Restatement (Second) of Contracts sections (the text is not freely hosted, so I cite sections and paraphrase them), the Federal Rules of Evidence and Civil Procedure and UCC Article 2 via Cornell LII, the ABA Model Rules, and the cases a business law casebook uses. I checked FRE 408, FRCP 30 and FRCP 68, and *Minneapolis & St. Louis Ry. v. Columbus Rolling-Mill*, against their current text on LII while writing this.

**Verdicts.**
- *Real error*: the copy states or implies something that is wrong in law, and nothing in the design needs it to be wrong.
- *Deliberate simplification — say so*: the mechanic departs from law on purpose, for a reason `CONTEXT.md` or an ADR gives, but no player copy says it is a simplification. A student will take it as law.
- *Accurate*: correct as written. Later rewrites should keep it correct.

## The terms of art, one by one

### Offer

**Law.** An offer is a manifestation of willingness to enter a bargain. It must be made so that the offeree is justified in understanding that their assent is invited and will close the deal (R2d §24). It gives the offeree a power of acceptance (§35), and it must be communicated to the offeree before it can be accepted. Its terms must be reasonably certain (§33).

**Game.** A committed Offer is a sound model of an offer: definite terms, communicated to the other Side, open to acceptance. The two client-side refusals, "An offer names at least one term." and "An offer of money is worth an amount.", are correct statements of definiteness. Two things are wrong. The copy calls the *making* of an offer "executing" it (#2). It also calls an unsent internal draft "on the table" (#3).

### Acceptance

**Law.** Acceptance is a manifestation of assent to the offer's terms, made in the manner the offer invites (§50). It must mirror the offer. A reply that adds or changes terms is not an acceptance but a counter-offer (§§58–59). UCC §2-207 relaxes this only for sales of goods, which a settlement of an employment claim is not (§2-102).

**Game.** Accurate. "Accept their offer" takes the whole instrument as drawn: there is no partial or conditional acceptance, and nothing is re-typed. "this closes Day n and settles the matter · there is nothing after it" is correct, because acceptance concludes the contract. Acceptance lands instantly for both Sides. That is consistent with the rule that treats instantaneous communication like face-to-face dealing (§64), so the mailbox rule (§63) never arises and nothing in the copy misstates it.

### Counteroffer

**Law.** A counter-offer is an offer on the same matter that proposes a substitute bargain (§39(1)). It ends the offeree's power to accept the original unless either side says otherwise (§39(2), §38). *Minneapolis & St. Louis Ry. v. Columbus Rolling-Mill* (1886) is the US Supreme Court's statement of the rule: a party that has rejected an offer by varying its terms cannot later revive it by accepting.

**Game.** The word "counteroffer" never appears in the copy. The redline ("Struck through, their last committed offer. Write ours on the same line.") is an accurate picture of how a counter is drafted. The departure is in what the counter does. In the game it leaves the original acceptable (#6). This is the single most teachable divergence in the game, because a student who counters and then accepts the original has done in play what the case law says cannot be done.

### An offer standing until taken

**Law.** An offer ends through rejection or counter-offer, lapse of time, revocation, or death or incapacity (§36). An offer that names a time lapses at that time. Otherwise it lapses after a reasonable time (§41). The offeror may revoke it at will before acceptance (§42), including by conduct the offeree learns of that is inconsistent with it (§43). Only an option contract (§§25, 87), or a merchant's firm offer for goods (UCC §2-205), is irrevocable.

**Game.** An Offer "stands on the table until it is taken or the run ends" (`CONTEXT.md` § Acceptance). That is an irrevocable, non-lapsing offer made without consideration. The design has a reason for it: one commit per Day, and a redline that shows the other Side's position whole. But no player copy says it is a game rule rather than law (#6). The seed makes things worse, because its note sets a deadline and the mechanic ignores it (#1).

### *Without prejudice*

**Law.** In England and the Commonwealth, "without prejudice" is the label that invokes settlement privilege. In the US, FRE 408 bars evidence of compromise offers and of statements made in compromise negotiations when they are offered to prove or disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim, or to impeach. It applies whatever the letter is labelled. Its exceptions (408(b)) allow such evidence for other purposes, such as bias. In US procedure, *without prejudice* ordinarily means "without loss of the right to refile" (FRCP 41).

**Game.** The phrase appears twice: the seed note and the placeholder that suggests it to every student (#4). There is a second reason to fix it. `CONTEXT.md` § Arbitration Packet plans a final-offer draft award, which puts the Sides' final Offers in front of the Instructor-as-arbitrator. That is the opposite of what a settlement-privilege label promises. Arbitration is not built yet, but when it is, the copy will need to say which offers the arbitrator sees.

### Exhibits being served

**Law.** Service is the formal delivery of litigation papers (FRCP 5). A settlement offer is not served unless it is a Rule 68 offer of judgment. An exhibit is a document marked in a proceeding (FRCP 10(c), 26(a)(3), 30(f)). Once a document has been handed over, the receiving side is free to use it.

**Game.** "Served", "Served on you", "Exhibit" and "rides the offer" belong to ADR 0005's paper register. They are a coherent metaphor, but none of them is law (#7, #8). The deposition that arrives by service is a separate problem, because depositions are attended by both sides (#9). One sentence is accurate and worth keeping: "It is how you learn you have been argued at — never what the argument was worth." It makes no legal claim.

### Countersignature

**Law.** Not a term with a fixed legal definition. It is a second signature confirming a first. In contract practice it often means the second *party's* signature.

**Game.** Used consistently for the intra-Team Second. Accurate in general usage (#12). The executed instrument's "Signed by" and "Countersigned by" lines per Side read as dual authorisation for one party, which is a real practice. The problem is the gap around them: no line exists for the Client, who is the one person with authority to settle (#10).

### Execution

**Law.** Executing an instrument means signing it (and delivering it) so that it takes effect. "Executed contract" also has a second, classroom sense: a contract fully *performed*, as opposed to an executory one. Signing is an objective manifestation of assent, whatever the signer privately meant (*Lucy v. Zehmer*, 196 Va. 493 (1954)).

**Game.** The executed instrument itself is described correctly: "Executed on Day n … These are the terms both Sides signed", "Executed by", "You are looking at the executed agreement." The error is using the same word for making an offer (#2). A course that teaches executed versus executory contracts will also find "executed" doing a third job here.

## Accurate as written: do not break these

- "Accept their offer"; "Confirm accepting their offer": acceptance of the whole instrument (R2d §§50, 58).
- "this closes Day {n} and settles the matter · there is nothing after it": acceptance concludes the bargain.
- "The matter has already settled. There is nothing left to accept.": an accepted offer cannot be accepted again.
- "An offer names at least one term." / "An offer of money is worth an amount.": definiteness (§33).
- "Draft terms of settlement", "Draft — not executed", "Not yet on the table" (the unsent-edits mark): a draft is not an offer.
- "Struck through, their last committed offer. Write ours on the same line.": how a counter is drafted. Only its *effect* in the game is simplified (#6).
- "No Term is on the table. Nothing here is zero — an offer of nothing is a position somebody took…": silence on a term is not an offer of zero.
- "Terms of settlement"; "Executed on Day {n}, {date}. These are the terms both Sides signed"; "Executed by"; "For the {Role}"; "You are looking at the executed agreement.": execution in its correct sense. Keep these even after #2 is fixed; they are the right home for the word.
- "Their offer, open on the table" and "The terms struck through on the sheet above, drawn by {name} and committed on Day {n}.": correct for a communicated offer.
- "Request documents" (FRCP 34), "Retain an expert", "Manage the press": accurate labels for real litigation work.
- "Useful to argue from; not a document to put in front of anyone." (the comparable-awards memo): a fair description of attorney work product (FRCP 26(b)(3)). Only its title and the Action that yields it are wrong (#5).
- Defendant: "closed without anyone admitting to something that did not happen": a no-admission settlement, consistent with FRE 408.
- Plaintiff: "I am the one who decided it was enough": the client decides whether to settle (Model Rule 1.2(a)). This is the line to build #10's fix around.

## Findings that should become their own tickets

1. **The seed's "Open for acceptance today" is accepted on a later Day (#1).** It needs a decision, which is why it is not a copy fix. One option: change the note so it names no deadline. The other: build the offer's own deadline into the game, which is a mechanic. Neither can be settled inside the voice rewrite.
2. **Say the standing-offer rule where the Day's grammar is named (#6).** `reads.morning_briefing.grammar` is the one line of copy that states the machine's rules (`CONTEXT.md` § Morning Briefing). That line, or the acceptance block, should say that in this game an offer stays open until it is accepted or the run ends, and that a counter does not withdraw it, which is not how offers work at law. This is the largest single gap between the game and the doctrine the course teaches.
3. **Rename the act of making an offer away from "execute" (#2, #3).** The word runs through the locale file, five components, the Docket act label and ADR 0005's vocabulary ("an Offer commits by executing a draft"). That makes it an ADR-level renaming, not a string edit. The rename should keep "executed" for the accepted instrument only.
4. **"Research precedent" yields settlements (#5).** Either rename the Action (for example "Research settlement values") or re-author the document as case law. The Action kind is an engine vocabulary, so this touches `reads.action_board.kinds` and the Case schema, not only prose.
5. **Settlement authority and the missing release (#10, #11).** Both belong in one short "what this instrument leaves out" statement on the executed instrument, or in the Rubric's front matter. It should say that a real settlement also carries the plaintiff's release and a dismissal, and needs the client's authority, and that the game assumes both.
6. **Forward-looking, for when Arbitration is built.** An arbitrator's authority comes from an agreement to arbitrate ([9 U.S.C. §2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/2)); arbitration does not follow automatically from failed negotiation, and the arbitrator is not a judge. A final-offer award also puts settlement offers before the decider, which clashes with FRE 408 (#4). The Arbitration copy will need its own legal pass.
